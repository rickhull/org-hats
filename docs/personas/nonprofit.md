# For an organization that doesn't have an engineering team

Everyone who helps your organization gets exactly the access their current
role needs, nothing more — checked automatically, not by someone remembering
to update a list.

## What that means day to day

The same volunteer can help two different programs and not carry the same
keys to both. If they're a coordinator at one site and just a helper at
another, the system knows the difference — because someone granted them
each role separately, not because they're generally "in" the organization.

## What happens when someone leaves

When a role is removed, it stops being usable on a short, predictable
timeline — it doesn't linger indefinitely, and it doesn't rely on everyone
remembering to update a spreadsheet. It also isn't instant the way flipping
a light switch is: there's a bounded window, not a promise of "the second it
happens." Someone still has to tell the system a person left — it doesn't
notice on its own.

## What you still have to do

This doesn't pull your staff and volunteer list from somewhere else
automatically. Someone on your team still adds people, assigns their roles,
and removes them when they leave — the same way someone already has to today.
What changes is that the rule about who can do what is explicit and checked
every time, instead of living in someone's memory or an old document.

## What this doesn't do

It doesn't manage your email, your donor database, or your case files — it
only answers "is this person allowed to do this, here." It doesn't require a
new identity system, but it does need a technical person to set it up and
keep it running; this page is about what it does for your organization, not
how to install it.
